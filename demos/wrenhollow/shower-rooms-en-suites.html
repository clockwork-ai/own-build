Small rooms, done properly.

Taking the bath out and putting a shower room in, or finding an en suite in a box room or over the stairs. Small rooms are less forgiving, so the setting out matters more, not less.

  • We never use the bath and we would rather have a decent shower.

  • Everything we like looks too big for the space.

On the job

What happens

  1. Measure the room properly, including the door swing and where you actually stand.
  2. Waste and water runs worked out first, because in a small room they decide the layout.
  3. Tray or enclosure set dead level and sealed, or the floor tanked if it is a wet room.
  4. Tiling set out so the cuts land in corners, not down the middle of the main wall.
  5. Compact suite fitted, extractor wired in by a qualified electrician, and the room tested with the shower running.

Questions

Common questions

Will taking the bath out hurt the house when we sell?

Honest answer: it depends who buys it. If it is your only bathroom and the house suits families, a lot of buyers still want a bath somewhere. If you have another bathroom with a bath in it, nobody minds. We will say what we think, but it is your call, not ours.

How small can an en suite be?

Smaller than most people expect, as long as the door swing and the waste run work. What kills it is not floor area, it is where the soil pipe is and whether the ceiling comes down over the stairs. We measure it and tell you yes, no, or yes but the door has to slide.

Do we need a pump?

Sometimes. If you are on a gravity system with a tank in the loft and the shower is close to it, the pressure may be poor, and a pump or a change of valve sorts it. If you are on a combi or an unvented cylinder, usually not. We check the pressure on the visit rather than guessing.

Can you put a shower over a bath instead?

Yes, and in a one bathroom house it is often the sensible answer. A glass screen instead of a curtain, a proper mixer valve and a flat bath with a decent standing area gets most of the benefit without losing the bath.

Want somebody to come and look?

Tell us what you are after and where you are. We will measure the room up and put it in writing.

Isolate the water first, then ring

Stop the water before you do anything else. Turn the shower or the tap off at the valve, and if it keeps coming, shut the stopcock, usually under the kitchen sink. Then ring and tell us what you have got and we will say honestly whether it needs somebody tonight or in the morning.

While you wait

  1. Nobody uses the shower, the bath or that toilet until we have seen it.
  2. Leave the lights and any socket near the wet patch alone. Switch that circuit off at the board if you can do it safely.
  3. Put a bowl under the drip and towels round it. If the ceiling is bulging, do not stand under it.
  4. Move anything that matters out from underneath, and lift the carpet edge if it is soaking.
  5. Take a couple of photos of the stain and of the bathroom floor. They help us work out where it is coming from.

If water is near a light fitting or a consumer unit and you are not sure, turn the power off and call an electrician or 999 before you call us.
