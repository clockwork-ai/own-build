On the job

What happens

  1. Check the wall and the floor are flat and sound, and say so if they are not.
  2. Dry lay and set out from a centre line, marking where every cut lands.
  3. Fix in notched adhesive, back buttering the big ones, with levelling clips on the joints.
  4. Leave it to set properly before grouting, rather than rushing it the same day.
  5. Grout, then sealant in the corners and the changes of plane, because grout cracks there and sealant does not.

Questions

Common questions

Why do big tiles need a flatter wall?

Because a large tile cannot follow a bumpy wall. Any hollow shows as lippage, one tile edge standing above its neighbour, and in a bathroom with a window the side light picks out every one. On a wall that is out, the honest answer is that it gets boarded or floated first.

Should the corners be grouted or sealed?

Sealed. Anywhere two planes meet moves slightly, and grout in that joint will crack and let water through. Grout goes in the field joints, sealant goes in the internal corners, round the tray and along the bath.

Can you tile over existing tiles?

Sometimes, if the old ones are sound, flat and well stuck, and the extra thickness does not foul the door or the window reveal. Often it is false economy, because you inherit whatever is wrong behind them. We will tell you which it is after a look.

Do you supply the tiles?

We will if you want, or fit what you have chosen. Order about ten per cent more than the area to cover cuts and breakages, and keep a few back for repairs, because the batch you buy today will not match in three years.

Want somebody to come and look?

Tell us what you are after and where you are. We will measure the room up and put it in writing.

Isolate the water first, then ring

Stop the water before you do anything else. Turn the shower or the tap off at the valve, and if it keeps coming, shut the stopcock, usually under the kitchen sink. Then ring and tell us what you have got and we will say honestly whether it needs somebody tonight or in the morning.

While you wait

  1. Nobody uses the shower, the bath or that toilet until we have seen it.
  2. Leave the lights and any socket near the wet patch alone. Switch that circuit off at the board if you can do it safely.
  3. Put a bowl under the drip and towels round it. If the ceiling is bulging, do not stand under it.
  4. Move anything that matters out from underneath, and lift the carpet edge if it is soaking.
  5. Take a couple of photos of the stain and of the bathroom floor. They help us work out where it is coming from.

If water is near a light fitting or a consumer unit and you are not sure, turn the power off and call an electrician or 999 before you call us.
