On the job

What happens

  1. Strip out: suite, tiles and flooring away, and we see what is behind them.
  2. Pipework and waste reworked for the new layout, pressure tested before anything covers it.
  3. Walls boarded or made good, floor levelled, and the room squared up for tiling.
  4. Tiling set out dry first, then fixed, then grouted and sealed.
  5. Suite, screen, taps and the towel rail fitted, the extractor wired in by a qualified electrician, and the room cleaned down.

Questions

Common questions

Can we move the toilet?

Usually, but it is the change that costs. The toilet has to fall to the soil pipe, so moving it means moving the waste run, and that means lifting the floor and sometimes going out through the wall. We will tell you on the visit whether your floor and your soil stack allow it, and what it adds.

Do you take the old suite away?

Yes. It goes out with us, along with the tiles and the flooring. On a real site this is where a firm would say how the waste is handled. The room is left swept at the end of every day, not just at the end of the job.

What happens if you find something behind the tiles?

On an older bathroom, something usually turns up: a rotten board under the bath, a patch of blown plaster, a joint somebody has bodged. We stop, show you it, tell you what it needs and what it adds, and get it agreed before we carry on. Nothing gets covered up quietly.

Do you take a deposit?

On a real site this is where a firm would set out its payment stages. This is a demo, so there is nothing to agree to and nothing to pay.

Want somebody to come and look?

Tell us what you are after and where you are. We will measure the room up and put it in writing.

Isolate the water first, then ring

Stop the water before you do anything else. Turn the shower or the tap off at the valve, and if it keeps coming, shut the stopcock, usually under the kitchen sink. Then ring and tell us what you have got and we will say honestly whether it needs somebody tonight or in the morning.

While you wait

  1. Nobody uses the shower, the bath or that toilet until we have seen it.
  2. Leave the lights and any socket near the wet patch alone. Switch that circuit off at the board if you can do it safely.
  3. Put a bowl under the drip and towels round it. If the ceiling is bulging, do not stand under it.
  4. Move anything that matters out from underneath, and lift the carpet edge if it is soaking.
  5. Take a couple of photos of the stain and of the bathroom floor. They help us work out where it is coming from.

If water is near a light fitting or a consumer unit and you are not sure, turn the power off and call an electrician or 999 before you call us.
