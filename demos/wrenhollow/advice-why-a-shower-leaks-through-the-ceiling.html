Why a shower leaks through the ceiling, and what to check

A stain downstairs almost never means the pipe has burst. Here are the usual suspects, in the order to check them.

A brown ring on the ceiling under a bathroom makes most people think a pipe has burst. Nine times out of ten it has not. Pipes leak all the time, whether anyone is in the house or not, so a leak that only shows after somebody showers is telling you it is not the pipework.

First: stop using it

Before you work out what it is, stop feeding it. Nobody uses that shower until it has been looked at. If it was the toilet, turn the valve on the cistern feed and leave it. Then put a bowl under the drip, keep away from any light fitting or socket near the wet patch, and switch that lighting circuit off at the board if you can do that safely.

Then work through them in this order

1. The sealant round the tray or the bath

The most common cause by a mile, and the cheapest. Look along the joint where the tray or the bath meets the tile. If it is split, lifting, black, or there is a gap you can get a fingernail into, water is going behind it every time somebody showers. Sealant also fails from behind when it was put on over a damp joint, so it can look fine from the front and be useless.

2. The screen or the door seal

Run the shower and watch the outside of the screen rather than the inside. If water tracks down the outside of the glass, or drips from the bottom of the frame onto the floor, the seal has gone or the screen is not sitting right. That water lands on the floor and finds the first gap in it.

3. The grout, especially in the corners

Grout in a vertical corner always cracks, because the two walls move independently. Cracked grout in the field of a tiled wall is bad enough, but in a corner or along the tray it is a direct route. Corners should be sealed, not grouted, and if yours are grouted that is probably your answer.

4. The waste connection under the tray

If the sealant and the screen check out, next is the waste. A tray waste that has worked loose, a shower trap that is not fully seated, or a pushfit joint that has been nudged will leak only when water is running through it. From underneath, the stain usually sits right below the tray rather than spread across the room.

5. The valve or the pipework in the wall

Last, because it is least likely and the most work to get at. A concealed mixer valve weeping behind the tile, or a joint in the wall, leaks when the shower is on and often shows as a damp patch on the wall below rather than a clean drip from the ceiling. If everything above has been ruled out properly, this is where the wall comes off, and not before.

Reading the stain

Touch it. A stain that is dry, hard edged and has been there for years is history, not a live leak. One that is damp, spreading, or got bigger after somebody showered is live. A bulge or a sag in the plaster means water is sitting in the ceiling void, and that one wants somebody out quickly before it comes down.

What not to do

  • Do not run the shower to see if it still does it. You already know.
  • Do not seal over a wet joint. It will not stick and it hides the evidence.
  • Do not poke a hole in the ceiling to drain it unless you have been asked to.
  • Do not redecorate the ceiling before the leak is found. The stain will come back through.

When to ring somebody

If water is near a light fitting, if the ceiling is sagging, or if it is still coming through after you have turned everything off, that is a now job. If it is a small damp ring that only appears after a shower, it is still worth sorting this week, because the thing that turns a sealant job into a new floor and a new ceiling is water getting under it for a year.

Written for this demo as general guidance. It is not advice on your bathroom, and products and rules change. Check anything that matters with somebody who has seen the room.

Isolate the water first, then ring

Stop the water before you do anything else. Turn the shower or the tap off at the valve, and if it keeps coming, shut the stopcock, usually under the kitchen sink. Then ring and tell us what you have got and we will say honestly whether it needs somebody tonight or in the morning.

While you wait

  1. Nobody uses the shower, the bath or that toilet until we have seen it.
  2. Leave the lights and any socket near the wet patch alone. Switch that circuit off at the board if you can do it safely.
  3. Put a bowl under the drip and towels round it. If the ceiling is bulging, do not stand under it.
  4. Move anything that matters out from underneath, and lift the carpet edge if it is soaking.
  5. Take a couple of photos of the stain and of the bathroom floor. They help us work out where it is coming from.

If water is near a light fitting or a consumer unit and you are not sure, turn the power off and call an electrician or 999 before you call us.
