On the job

What happens

  1. Check the carcasses first: square, solid, and hinges still holding where they are screwed.
  2. Measure every door and drawer front, because a kitchen is rarely as standard as it looks.
  3. Old doors off and the frames cleaned up, with any soft or broken carcass sorted before anything new goes on.
  4. New doors, drawer fronts and end panels fitted and adjusted so the gaps are even across the run.
  5. New worktop if it is part of it, then handles, plinths and a final set of adjustments once everything is hung.

Questions

Common questions

How do I know if my carcasses are worth keeping?

Open a base unit and press the back and the bottom. If it is firm, dry and square, it is worth keeping. If the bottom has swollen under the sink, the back is coming away or the screws spin in the chipboard, you are putting new doors on something that is failing. We will tell you which it is.

Will new doors fit my old units?

Usually, but not always. Most British kitchens use fairly standard widths and hinge positions, so the doors can be made to suit. Older or custom kitchens sometimes need sizes made to measure. We measure every opening rather than assuming.

How long does it take?

Two to three days is normal for doors, handles and a worktop on an average kitchen. You keep your sink and your appliances the whole time, which is the real difference compared with a full fit.

Can I change the layout at the same time?

A little. Swapping a cupboard for a set of drawers is often doable. Moving the sink or the cooker is not, because at that point you are into a full fit and it is better to be honest about that at the start.

Water under the sink, or an appliance dead?

Turn the water off first, then ring

Fitting kitchens is not an emergency trade, so there is no night rota here. What we do have is a line for the two things that will not wait: a leak under a sink we plumbed, and an appliance we fitted that has stopped. Turn the water off at the isolating valve under the sink, or the stopcock if you cannot find it, then ring and tell us what is happening.

While you wait

  1. Empty the cupboard under the sink so the chipboard base can dry out.
  2. Put a bowl under the drip and a towel round it, and keep an eye on it.
  3. Leave the socket behind the appliance alone if anything near it is wet. Switch that circuit off at the board if you can do it safely.
  4. Stop using the appliance. Running it again to see if it still does it usually makes the mess bigger.
  5. Take a photo of where the water is coming from. It is often the easiest way for us to tell you what it is.

If water is anywhere near a socket or the consumer unit and you are not sure, turn the power off and call an electrician before you call us.
