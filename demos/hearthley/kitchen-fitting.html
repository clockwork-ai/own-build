On the job

What happens

  1. Strip out: old units, worktops and flooring away, and we see what is behind them.
  2. Walls made good, floor levelled, and the water, waste and power sorted for the new layout.
  3. Base units set out, levelled and scribed to the wall, with the run checked end to end.
  4. Worktops cut, jointed and fitted, or templated for stone and fitted a week or so later.
  5. Sink, taps and appliances in and tested, then doors, handles, plinths and cornice, and the room cleaned down.

Questions

Common questions

Can we move the sink?

Usually, but it is the change that adds time. The sink needs a waste that falls to the drain, so moving it means a new waste run, sometimes under the floor and sometimes out through the wall. We will tell you on the visit whether your floor and your drain allow it, and what it adds.

Will we be without a sink all week?

Not if we can help it. On most fits the old sink stays in use until the day it has to come out, and the new one goes in with a temporary waste as soon as the units are level. There is normally a day in the middle where you cannot use it, and we tell you which day that is before we start.

What happens if you find something behind the units?

On an older kitchen, something usually turns up: a soft floorboard under the sink, a patch of damp plaster, a bit of wiring somebody has buried. We stop, show you it, say what it needs and what it adds, and get it agreed before we carry on. Nothing gets covered up quietly.

Do you take a deposit?

On a real site this is where a firm would set out its payment stages. This is a demo, so there is nothing to agree to and nothing to pay.

Want somebody to come and measure up?

Tell us what you are after and where you are. We will measure the room and put it in writing.

Water under the sink, or an appliance dead?

Turn the water off first, then ring

Fitting kitchens is not an emergency trade, so there is no night rota here. What we do have is a line for the two things that will not wait: a leak under a sink we plumbed, and an appliance we fitted that has stopped. Turn the water off at the isolating valve under the sink, or the stopcock if you cannot find it, then ring and tell us what is happening.

While you wait

  1. Empty the cupboard under the sink so the chipboard base can dry out.
  2. Put a bowl under the drip and a towel round it, and keep an eye on it.
  3. Leave the socket behind the appliance alone if anything near it is wet. Switch that circuit off at the board if you can do it safely.
  4. Stop using the appliance. Running it again to see if it still does it usually makes the mess bigger.
  5. Take a photo of where the water is coming from. It is often the easiest way for us to tell you what it is.

If water is anywhere near a socket or the consumer unit and you are not sure, turn the power off and call an electrician before you call us.
