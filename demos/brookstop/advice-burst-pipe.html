What to do if a pipe bursts

Five steps to take in the first ten minutes, before anyone arrives.

A burst pipe is stressful, but the first few minutes make a big difference. Do these in order.

1. Turn off the stopcock

Usually under the kitchen sink, sometimes in a downstairs loo or where the pipe enters the house. Turn it clockwise until it stops. Find yours now, before you need it.

2. Keep water away from the electrics

If water is near sockets, lights or the fuse board, switch off at the consumer unit, but only if you can reach it from somewhere dry. If you can't, keep away and call for help.

3. Drain the system

Open the cold taps and flush the toilets to empty the pipes. If you have a hot water cylinder, switch off the immersion and the boiler.

4. Contain the water

Buckets, towels, and move what you can out of the way. Take photos for your insurer.

5. Call a plumber

Tell them where the leak is and what you've done so far. It helps us bring the right parts.

General information only. Every home is different, so speak to a qualified engineer about yours. Never work on gas appliances yourself.

While you wait

  1. Turn off the water at your stopcock. It's usually under the kitchen sink, or where the pipe comes into the house.
  2. If water is near sockets, lights or the fuse board, switch the electrics off at the consumer unit, but only if you can reach it from somewhere dry.
  3. Open the cold taps to drain the pipes, and catch drips with towels and buckets.
  4. Move anything valuable out of the way, then call us on the number above.

Smell gas? Don't use switches or flames. Open doors and windows, leave the property and call the National Gas Emergency number on 0800 111 999.
