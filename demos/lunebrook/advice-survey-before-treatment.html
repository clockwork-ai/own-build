Why a survey should come before any treatment

Treating the wrong problem costs money and doesn't fix the damp. Here's what a proper survey looks at.

Damp is a symptom. The cause can be the ground, the gutters, the walls, the way a house is heated and aired, or a leak nobody has spotted yet. Treating the symptom without finding the cause rarely lasts.

What a good survey looks at

  • Inside: where the damp is, how high it goes, and the readings in each room.
  • Outside: ground levels, gutters, downpipes, pointing, sills and airbricks.
  • Under the floor where we can: air flow and the state of the timbers.
  • How the house is lived in: heating, drying washing, extractor fans.

What you should get

A written report in plain English, with photos, that tells you what's causing the damp and what to do about it, in order. Sometimes the answer is a simple outside fix and no treatment at all.

A word on meters

Moisture meters are useful, but they can be fooled by salts and some materials. A good surveyor uses them as one clue among several, not as the whole answer.

General information only. Please don't buy or inject any treatment without a survey. Speak to a qualified surveyor about your home.

Three quick clues

  1. Worse in winter, on windows and in corners, with black spots? Often condensation.
  2. A tide mark low on a ground floor wall, with crumbly or salty plaster? Could be rising damp, or water from outside.
  3. Small round holes in timber with fine dust underneath? Worth having woodworm checked.

These are clues, not a diagnosis. Plenty of damp has more than one cause, which is why we survey before anyone treats anything.
