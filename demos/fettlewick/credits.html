Where the pictures came from

Every picture on this site was generated for this demo. None of them is a photograph of a real job, a real house or a real person, and there is no real company name, logo or van anywhere in any of them.

How they were made

Twenty two scenes were written out one by one as plain descriptions of an ordinary British house in Sefton, then generated as images. Every scene was written to carry no text at all: no signage, no brand names, no printed labels, no numbers on a tape or a level, nothing written on a bag or a tin.

Where a person appears, it is a pair of hands or somebody seen from directly behind. No face, no profile and nobody recognisable, because inventing a tradesman's face for a firm that does not exist is a line worth not crossing.

Ten of the twenty two were generated twice. The first set of indoor scenes came back with a stack of fence panels leaning in the hallway, the kitchen and the bedroom, because the description of the trade's materials mentioned fence timber and that dragged it into every room. The second run used a description written for indoor work only, and the fence timber went back outside where it belongs.

The after picture in the before and after slider was made from the before picture, so it is the same fence in the same garden from the same spot rather than two different gardens.

Sizes

Each picture is saved at several widths as WebP, and the page asks for the one that suits the screen. Nothing on the site is over two hundred kilobytes.

The ones worth ringing about

  • A door or a window that will not shut or lock, so the house cannot be left
  • A handrail, a banister or a grab rail come loose on the stairs
  • A gate or a fence panel down where a dog or a child can get out
  • Something heavy on a wall that has started to pull away from it
  • A floorboard, a step or a threshold strip somebody is catching a foot on

These need a different trade, today

Anything on the heating system, water coming through a ceiling, or a socket or a light that has started tripping or scorching. Ring a heating engineer, a plumber or an electrician for those. We would rather say so on the phone than take the job and leave you waiting.
