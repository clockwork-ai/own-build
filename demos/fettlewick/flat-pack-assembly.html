Flat pack

Flat pack assembly

Wardrobes, beds, desks, drawer units and the thing that has been in its box since August. Built square, stood where you want it, and the cardboard folded and taken away.

What this coversOn the list

  1. Wardrobes and sliding door unitsThe ones where square matters most, because a door that will not slide is almost always a carcase that is out of true rather than a faulty door.
  2. Beds, bunks and divan framesIncluding the ones with drawers that only line up if the frame goes together in the right order.
  3. Desks, drawer units and shelvingBuilt, levelled on an uneven floor and stood where you actually want it rather than where the box was.
  4. Kitchen and bathroom cabinetsFlat pack carcases assembled and hung. Hanging means fixings, so the wall gets checked first.
  5. Garden furniture and shedsFlat pack outdoor sets, and the small shed kits that come as five panels and a bag of screws.
  6. Anti tip straps and wall fixingsTall units strapped to the wall. We do this as a matter of course on anything a child could climb.

Drawn, not photographed

What fits in a visit

Flat pack is the clearest case for sending a list. One wardrobe and the setup is paid for one wardrobe. Three items and a bed, and it is paid once.

Square is the whole job

Flat pack goes wrong in one of two places. The first is the order of assembly, which the instructions usually do get right and people usually do not follow. The second is square, and that is the one that bites later.

A carcase that is a few millimetres out of true will stand up fine and look fine. Then the doors will not line up, the sliding doors will catch at one end, and the drawers will run on one side and not the other. Checking the diagonals before the back goes on takes a minute, and the back is what locks it in, so after that minute it is too late.

Floors are never flat

Especially in older houses round here. A tall unit stood on a sloping floor will lean, and a leaning wardrobe makes its own doors swing shut. Packers under the base take that out before anything is loaded into it.

The strap is not optional

Anything tall and narrow gets strapped to the wall. Chests of drawers and wardrobes tip when the top drawer is out and something climbs on it, and the strap in the bag is there for exactly that. We fix it to something that will hold it, which depends on what the wall is made of.

Missing and damaged parts

If a panel is chipped in the box or a bag of cams is short, we will tell you before we start rather than after, so you can get the replacement sent while the rest goes together. We carry ordinary screws and dowels, but cam locks are specific to the unit.

About this sort of job

Do you take the packaging?

Yes, cardboard and polystyrene from what we build goes with us.

What if a part is missing?

You will hear about it before we start. We can usually build everything else and leave the one piece until the replacement arrives.

Can you move the old one out?

Taking an old flat pack unit apart and out, yes, if it is on the list. We are not a house clearance though.
