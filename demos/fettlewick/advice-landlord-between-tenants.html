Small jobs landlords should sort between tenants

The week the keys are back is the only week the whole place is empty and nothing is urgent. Here is the list that is worth doing in it, and the order to do it in.

There is exactly one week in a tenancy cycle when a property is empty, nothing is urgent, nobody has to be asked for access and everything is reachable. Almost every small job is cheaper and easier in that week than in any other, and most of them never get done at all.

Start with the things an inventory picks up

Filled and sanded holes where pictures and brackets were. Curtain poles and blind brackets either refitted properly or taken off and the holes made good. Handles tight. Internal doors that shut without a shove. Bath and shower seals cut out and run again, because grey silicone photographs badly and reads as neglect.

None of it is expensive. All of it is in the first few pictures anybody looks at.

Then the safety related ones

Handrails and banisters that have worked loose. Grab rails in a bathroom. Window restrictors that have come off. Thresholds and carpet edges somebody could catch a foot on. Smoke alarm brackets where the alarm has been pulled off and put back badly.

These are the ones where doing it in the empty week is not just convenient. They are the jobs that become a reported repair, or worse, if they are left.

Doors are worth a pass on their own

Go round and shut every door, including the cupboards. The ones that need a shove have usually dropped rather than swollen, and a dropped door is often a ten minute job at the hinge. Doing all of them in one visit costs very little and removes a whole category of complaint.

Outside, while it is light

Gates that drag, a fence panel out, a shed roof letting water in, a loose slab by the back door. A tenant will rarely report any of these and all of them get worse over a winter.

What to book separately, now rather than later

Anything on the heating system, any electrical work, anything needing a certificate and anything on a roof. The empty week is also the easiest week for those trades to get access, so it is worth having the dates in hand before the keys go out again.

Write it down once and reuse it

The same list works for every property you have. Each visit comes back with the lines ticked, the lines not done and the reason, and that becomes the record for the next changeover. Over a few tenancies it stops being a list and starts being a history of the place.

And the one nobody does

Label the stopcock and the consumer unit, and check the loft hatch opens. A tenant who can find the stopcock in a hurry saves you a ceiling.

Questions

On this one

Can you do this with a tenant still in?

Yes, with their agreement and a time arranged with them. It is simply slower and more of it waits.

Do I need to be there?

No. Somebody needs to let us in, or an agent key release, and you get the write up either way.

Will you tell me what needs a specialist?

Yes, line by line, with the trade named. That is the part of the write up landlords use most.

The ones worth ringing about

  • A door or a window that will not shut or lock, so the house cannot be left
  • A handrail, a banister or a grab rail come loose on the stairs
  • A gate or a fence panel down where a dog or a child can get out
  • Something heavy on a wall that has started to pull away from it
  • A floorboard, a step or a threshold strip somebody is catching a foot on

These need a different trade, today

Anything on the heating system, water coming through a ceiling, or a socket or a light that has started tripping or scorching. Ring a heating engineer, a plumber or an electrician for those. We would rather say so on the phone than take the job and leave you waiting.
