Landlord work

Landlord and letting repairs

A list for a property between tenants, or a single repair reported by somebody living there now. One visit, one plain write up, and a note of anything that needs a specialist.

What this coversOn the list

  1. Between tenants listsThe empty week is the only week nothing is urgent. Filling, doors, handles, seals, blinds and the small things that get marked on an inventory.
  2. Reported repairs in occupied homesAppointment agreed with the tenant first, we knock rather than let ourselves in, and you get a write up the same day.
  3. Making good after a tenancyHoles filled and sanded, curtain brackets taken off or put back, loose fittings secured, and a tidy finish for photographs.
  4. Safety related small jobsLoose handrails and banisters, grab rails, window restrictors where they have come off, smoke alarm brackets, and thresholds people trip on.
  5. Outside jobs between lettingsGates, fence panels, a shed roof, a loose slab by the back door, and the gutters at ground level reachable without a tall ladder.
  6. Written up, job by jobEach line ticked, each one that was not done written down with the reason and the trade it needs.

What goes elsewherePassed on

  1. Compliance certificatesSafety checks and certificates come from the trade that is allowed to issue them. We do not issue any paperwork of that kind.
  2. Heating and electrical faultsStraight to the right trade, same day, and we will tell you which one it is.
  3. Full refurbishmentsA whole flat stripped and redone is a builder and a programme of trades, not a handyman with a list.

The write up after a visit uses exactly this split, so you can see at a glance what still needs booking.

The empty week is the cheap week

Every small job in an occupied property costs an appointment, a tenant's afternoon and a bit of goodwill. The same job between tenants costs none of those things. That is the whole argument for doing a list the week the keys come back, and it is why the list is usually worth more than it looks.

It is also the only week anybody can get at the wall behind the wardrobe.

Working round a tenant

When somebody is living there, the appointment is agreed with them, not around them. We confirm a window with the tenant, turn up inside it, knock, take shoes off or cover them, work clean and leave the place as we found it.

If the tenant is not in, we do not let ourselves in unless you have told us in writing that they have agreed to it. That is their home for as long as they are in it.

The write up

You get the same list back with each line marked: done, not done, or passed on. The passed on ones say which trade and what to ask for. No invoice language, no padding, nothing that needs decoding on a train.

The jobs worth doing before the photographs

Filled and sanded holes, a front door that opens without a shove, blinds that go up and down, handles that are tight, seals that are white rather than grey. None of it is expensive and all of it is in the first three pictures anybody looks at.

About this sort of job

Can you deal with the tenant directly?

Yes, for arranging a time and getting in. Anything about the tenancy itself goes back to you.

Do you work for agents as well as landlords?

Yes, on the same terms: a list, a visit and a plain write up.

Can you certify anything?

No. Certificates come from the trades allowed to issue them, and we will tell you which one you need.

The ones worth ringing about

  • A door or a window that will not shut or lock, so the house cannot be left
  • A handrail, a banister or a grab rail come loose on the stairs
  • A gate or a fence panel down where a dog or a child can get out
  • Something heavy on a wall that has started to pull away from it
  • A floorboard, a step or a threshold strip somebody is catching a foot on

These need a different trade, today

Anything on the heating system, water coming through a ceiling, or a socket or a light that has started tripping or scorching. Ring a heating engineer, a plumber or an electrician for those. We would rather say so on the phone than take the job and leave you waiting.
