What we do

Six kinds of job, and one habit under all of them.

Whatever the job is, most of it happens before any paint. Fill, caulk, sand, dust off, then the right first coat for what the surface actually is. Get that wrong and the best paint in the shop still looks like a rushed job in a fortnight. Here is each one on its own.

One at a time.

The coats

What a paint job is actually made of.

Four parts, and only the last one is the bit people picture. Where a job goes wrong is nearly always one of the first three.

The surfacePlaster, paper, old paint, bare timber or renderWhat is there now. It decides the primer, it decides whether anything can go straight on, and it is the only part of the job you cannot change your mind about later.
The making goodFill, caulk, sand, dust offHoles and dents filled, every gap along the skirting and the architrave caulked, the lot sanded flat and then the dust taken off properly. Nearly all of the finish is made here, before any paint exists.
The first coatMist coat, primer or undercoatOn new plaster a watered down mist coat so it soaks in. On bare or patchy timber a primer. On a strong colour you are covering, an undercoat or a tinted coat so the top coats are not doing the hiding.
The two top coatsThe bit people think is the whole jobTwo thin even coats rather than one thick one, each laid off the same way, with the cutting in done fresh so the brush and the roller blend instead of framing the wall.

What we do not do

Worth saying out loud.

Paint over damp

A tide mark low down, salts coming through, paint bubbling off in plates or black mould in a corner means something is wet and the painting is not the job yet. Paint seals damp in, it never cures it, and it comes back through whatever goes on top of it.

Paint straight onto bare new plaster

New plaster drinks. Full strength emulsion straight onto it gets the water pulled out of it faster than it can form a film, and it peels later in sheets. It wants a watered down mist coat first, and it wants to be dry before even that.

Promise a finish the surface cannot give

A door with forty years of brush marks in it will not come up like a sprayed panel from two coats, and painted units will not look like a new kitchen up close. We will tell you what it will look like before you spend the money rather than after.

Decorating is not an emergency trade, and we will not pretend it is

Nobody needs a decorator at two in the morning. What people do need, fairly often, is a room or a flat turned round in a short window: a tenancy ending, a house going on the market, a landlord with a void week, a shop shut for two days. That is the job this is about.

What makes it possible

  1. An empty room, or one that can be emptied. Working round furniture is what turns one day into two.
  2. Plain colours. A tenancy white throughout goes on faster than four shades and a feature wall.
  3. Sensible expectations about the woodwork. Oil based paint on a cold week is slow whatever anybody promises.
  4. Honest photographs sent through first, so we know what the walls are before anybody turns up.

What we will not do

Promise you a time on a web page. We do not publish a response time, because any number we put here would be made up. Ring, tell us the date you need it done by, and you will get a yes or a no rather than a maybe.

If there is water coming through a ceiling or paint bubbling off a damp wall, that is not a decorating job yet. Get the leak and the damp sorted first, or the new paint goes the same way as the old.
