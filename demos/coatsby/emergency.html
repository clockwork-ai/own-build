Quick turnaround

Keys back on Friday?

Decorating is not an emergency trade and we are not going to pretend it is. Nobody needs a painter at two in the morning. What people genuinely do need, fairly often, is a room or a flat turned round in a short window: a tenancy ending, a house going on the market, a landlord with a void week, a shop shut for two days. Here is what makes that work and what quietly ruins it.

What actually decides it

Empty room, plain colours, honest date.

This makes it possible

  1. An empty room or an empty flat. Furniture out, carpets lifted or protected, nothing left to work around.
  2. Plain colours throughout. One white on the walls and one finish on the woodwork goes on far faster than four shades and a feature wall.
  3. Clear photographs sent through first, from every corner of each room, so we know what the walls are before anybody turns up.
  4. A real date rather than a hopeful one. Tell us the day the keys go back and we will tell you yes or no.
  5. Water based paint on the woodwork where it suits, because it will take two coats in a day where oil based will not.

This is what eats the days

  • A flat that is still half full on the morning we arrive. Moving somebody else's furniture is the single biggest delay there is.
  • Damp, a leak or bubbling paint found on day one. That is not a decorating job yet, and painting over it wastes the money twice.
  • A strong colour being covered with a pale one, which needs an extra coat nobody allowed for.
  • Oil based gloss in a cold week. It wants overnight between coats and it will not be hurried by anybody.
  • Decisions still being made. Picking a colour on the Wednesday of a four day job costs a day.

The three we get rung about

What each one really needs.

End of tenancy

A void week between tenants, and the aim is a clean even flat rather than a showpiece. Plain throughout, the same specification every time, marks and hook holes filled, and the woodwork touched in rather than stripped. The thing that makes or breaks it is whether the flat is actually empty on the first morning, and whether the carpets are going in before or after.

Going on the market

A house about to be photographed. Here the money goes furthest on the hall, the stairs and the landing, because that is what everybody sees first and it is the bit most often left. Quiet neutral colours, the front door done, and the woodwork crisp. Bold colours get covered, not because they are wrong, but because a buyer is pricing the repaint.

A shop or an office over a shutdown

Two days closed, or a weekend, or the quiet fortnight. Planned round the trading hours rather than ours, in water based paint where we can so the smell goes overnight. The test is whether the place can open on Monday with nothing taped up, nothing left down and no dust on anybody's desk.

Why we will not put a time on this page

Every firm you ring will tell you they can turn a flat round quickly. The honest answer is that it depends on the size of it, the state of it, whether it is empty, and what else is booked that week. A one bedroom flat in sound condition and a three bedroom house with a stripped bathroom are not the same job, and no number printed on a web page covers both.

So there is no response time anywhere on this site, and no turnaround badge with a number on it. Ring, tell us the property and the date, and you will get a yes or a no rather than a maybe that turns into a no on the Thursday.

What we will tell you straight away

Whether it fits. Whether it fits if the colours are simplified. What we would leave out to make the date, and what we would honestly rather not leave out. And if we cannot do it, we will say that on the phone rather than taking a deposit and hoping.

The one thing that is not a decorating job

Water coming through a ceiling, paint bubbling off in plates, a tide mark low down a wall, or black mould in a corner. All of those mean something is wet. Paint over it and the new paint goes exactly the way the old paint went, usually inside a few months, and the money is gone twice.

Get the cause found and fixed, let the wall dry out, and then decorate it. On a rental that conversation is better had with the agent now than after the next tenant has moved in.

If you are a landlord with more than one

The biggest saving is not the cheapest quote. It is keeping one written specification across every property: the same white on the walls, the same finish on the woodwork, the same filler. Then a scuffed wall in two years is one wall touched in rather than a room repainted because nobody can match what was there.

Questions

Asked when the date is tight.

Can you do my flat this week?

On this demo nobody can, because the firm is not real and the number rings nobody. On a real site the answer depends on three things: the size of it, whether it is empty, and what is already booked.

What a real firm should do is ask you those three questions on the phone and give you a straight yes or no the same day, rather than a maybe that turns into a no when you have run out of time to ring anybody else.

How fast can a flat be turned round?

There is no number on this page on purpose. A small flat in sound condition, empty, plain colours throughout, is a very different job to the same flat with furniture still in it, a strong colour to cover and woodwork that wants taking back.

Send photographs of every room from the corners, say the date the keys go back, and you will get an honest answer rather than an average.

Is it cheaper if I do the prep myself?

Sometimes, and we will tell you where it genuinely helps. Clearing the flat out, taking the curtain poles and the hooks down and lifting the carpets saves real time and we will price it lower.

Filling and sanding is different. If it has been done well it saves a bit. If it has been done badly we have to take it back off first, which costs more than if nobody had touched it. If you want to help, do the clearing rather than the filling.

Can you work evenings or weekends?

For commercial work that is usually how it goes, and we would rather plan for it properly than squeeze a job into a trading day. For houses it is less common but not impossible, and it depends on the week.

Water based paint makes out of hours work far easier, because the smell has gone by the morning rather than hanging about.

What if you find something when you start?

You get told that day, before anything gets painted over. Damp behind a wardrobe, a ceiling that has been patched and never sealed, woodwork that is soft at the bottom of a frame. We will say what it means for the date and what the options are.

What we will not do is paint over it quietly so the job stays on schedule. That is the version everybody finds out about six months later.

Tell us the property, the rooms and the day the keys go back, and you will get a straight yes or no.
