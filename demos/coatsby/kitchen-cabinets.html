Kitchen cabinet painting

Kitchen cabinet painting.

Doors off, degreased properly, keyed, primed for the surface they actually are, then two thin coats. A kitchen that looks new for a fraction of a new kitchen, as long as everybody knows what it will and will not be.

Get a quote

What it can do, and what it cannot

Painting units changes a kitchen more than almost anything else for the money. A tired oak or a dated cream becomes a quiet modern colour, the handles get changed, and the room reads as new. From across the room it is transformed.

Up close it is painted furniture rather than a factory finish, and it should be sold to you that way. A brushed and rolled finish has a very fine texture to it. Door edges, the area round the handles and the cupboard under the sink take the wear first, because that is where hands and water go. It is a hard wearing finish, not an indestructible one, and the odd touch up in a few years is part of the deal.

Grease is the whole battle

Kitchen doors are coated in a film of cooking grease, including the ones that look clean, and especially the ones near the hob and above the kettle. Paint will not stick to it. No primer in the world fixes a greasy door.

So they get washed with a proper degreaser, rinsed, and washed again where they need it. Then keyed with abrasive so there is a surface for the primer to hold. The primer depends on what the doors actually are: solid timber, veneer, foil wrapped board and melamine all behave differently, and the wrong primer on foil wrap is the usual reason a painted kitchen fails.

How the week goes

Doors and drawer fronts come off and get numbered so every one goes back where it came from. The carcasses and the frames get done in place with everything masked off. The doors get done on trestles, both sides, with proper drying time between coats.

You keep the kitchen the whole time, which surprises people. The worktop, the sink, the oven and the hob all work. What you lose is the doors for a few days, so everything inside the cupboards is on show and anything on the worktop needs to be somewhere else. Thin coats and real drying time between them are what makes the finish hard, and there is no way of hurrying that part.

On the job

How this one usually goes.

  1. Doors off and numberedEvery door and drawer front off, hinges off, each one numbered on the inside edge so it goes back on the hinge it came off.
  2. Degrease, rinse and degrease againProper degreaser, not washing up liquid, over every face and edge, with extra attention round the hob, the kettle and the bin cupboard.
  3. Key and maskThe whole surface keyed with abrasive so the primer has something to hold, then the carcasses, the worktop, the floor and the splashback masked off.
  4. Prime for what they really areTimber, veneer, melamine and foil wrapped board each want a different primer. Getting this wrong is the usual reason a painted kitchen fails in a year.
  5. Two thin coats, properly driedTwo thin coats rather than one thick one, with real drying time between, then the doors rehung, adjusted level, and the new handles on.

A sample running order for this demo. Yours depends on the property and what the surfaces turn out to be doing.

Questions

Asked about this job.

Will it look like a new kitchen?

From across the room, yes. Up close it is painted furniture, with a very fine texture to it rather than a glass smooth sprayed panel. We would rather you knew that at the quote than noticed it afterwards.

How long will it last?

On well prepared doors, years of ordinary use. The edges, the handle areas and the cupboard under the sink wear first, because that is where hands and water are. Keep a little paint back and a touch up takes minutes.

Can you paint any kind of door?

Most of them: solid timber, veneer, melamine and foil wrapped board can all be painted with the right primer. Foil wrap is the one that needs the most care, because where the foil has lifted at an edge, paint will not hold it back down.

Do I lose the kitchen?

You lose the doors for a few days, not the kitchen. The oven, the hob, the sink and the worktop all carry on working. Clearing the worktops and accepting that the cupboards are open for a while is the whole inconvenience.

Can you change the handles?

Yes, and it is worth doing at the same time. If the new handles use different centres we will fill and redrill while the doors are off and bare, which is the only sensible time to do it.

Decorating is not an emergency trade, and we will not pretend it is

Nobody needs a decorator at two in the morning. What people do need, fairly often, is a room or a flat turned round in a short window: a tenancy ending, a house going on the market, a landlord with a void week, a shop shut for two days. That is the job this is about.

What makes it possible

  1. An empty room, or one that can be emptied. Working round furniture is what turns one day into two.
  2. Plain colours. A tenancy white throughout goes on faster than four shades and a feature wall.
  3. Sensible expectations about the woodwork. Oil based paint on a cold week is slow whatever anybody promises.
  4. Honest photographs sent through first, so we know what the walls are before anybody turns up.

What we will not do

Promise you a time on a web page. We do not publish a response time, because any number we put here would be made up. Ring, tell us the date you need it done by, and you will get a yes or a no rather than a maybe.

If there is water coming through a ceiling or paint bubbling off a damp wall, that is not a decorating job yet. Get the leak and the damp sorted first, or the new paint goes the same way as the old.
