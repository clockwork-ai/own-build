Repair or a new roof? How to tell

Most roofs don't need replacing. Here's how we decide, and what to look for from the ground.

We get asked this more than anything else. The honest answer is that most roofs can be repaired, at least for a while. Here's what tips it one way or the other.

Signs a repair will do

  • A few slipped or missing slates after a storm.
  • One leak in one place.
  • A ridge tile or two that's come loose.

Signs it may be time for a new roof

  • Slates slipping all over, not just in one patch.
  • Lots of old repairs: different coloured slates, tin patches, bitumen.
  • Sagging along the ridge or between the rafters.
  • Damp or daylight in the loft in several places.

Check from the ground

Stand back on the pavement or garden with your phone camera and zoom in. Never go up a ladder to look. Send us the photos and we'll tell you what we can see.

General information only. Never go on a roof yourself. Speak to a roofer about yours.

Safe first

  1. Don't go up a ladder or onto the roof. Not even to have a look.
  2. Inside, catch drips with buckets and move things out of the way.
  3. If water is near lights, sockets or the fuse board, switch off at the consumer unit, but only from somewhere dry.
  4. From the ground, take a few photos of the roof and any slates that have come down, then call us.

If anything's hanging loose over a path or the road, keep people well away from it and call us straight away.
