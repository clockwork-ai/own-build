The method

What a tiled job is actually made of.

Four parts, and only the last one is the bit people picture. Where a tiled wall goes wrong is nearly always one of the first three.

The backgroundFlat, dry, sound and primedWhat the tile is going onto. Flat enough for the size of tile you have chosen, firm enough not to flex, dry, and primed so the wall does not suck the water out of the adhesive before it has gone off. Nearly every tiling failure anybody ever shows us started here.
The waterproofingWhere water actually stopsIn a shower or a wet area, a proper waterproof layer behind the tile, taped at every corner and joint. Tile and grout are not a waterproof layer and were never meant to be. This is the part nobody ever sees and the part that decides whether the wall behind is still sound in ten years.
The set outWhere the cuts are going to landCentre lines struck, the course heights worked out, the awkward corners looked at and a decision made about which wall tells the truth. Twenty minutes with a tape before anything is mixed, and it is what separates a tiled wall from a tiled wall worth looking at.
The fixing, the grout and the sealThe part people pictureAdhesive combed out and the tile pressed into it with a full bed behind it, not five dabs. Joints the same all the way across. Grout in the flat joints, flexible sealant in every internal corner and every junction that moves. Then left alone long enough to go off properly.

From stripped to grouted

Five stages a tiled wall goes through.

Drawn rather than photographed. It is the same five whether it is a shower, a splashback or a kitchen floor.

StrippedOld tile off, old adhesive knocked back, and the first honest look at what is actually behind it. This is the day the job sometimes changes.
PreppedMade flat, made sound, made dry and primed. Boarded and waterproofed where it is going to get wet. Dull work, and it decides everything after it.
Set outCentre lines struck, course heights worked out, a batten screwed on to work up from, and the cuts put where the room looks at them least.
FixedAdhesive combed out, full bed behind every tile, levelling clips in while it goes off. Joints the same all the way across.
GroutedJoints filled and cleaned off, internal corners and every junction sealed with a flexible bead, then left alone long enough to cure properly.

The four we fix most, photographed from the edge so you can see what each one actually is. Left to right: ceramic, porcelain, natural slate and mosaic on mesh.

What we do not do

Worth saying out loud.

Tile over a wall that drums

If the old tile sounds hollow, the bed behind it has already let go. Sticking more weight on it does not fix that, it hurries it along. It comes off first, every time, even when that is not the answer anybody wanted on the day.

Grout an internal corner

Corners move. Grout does not. Every internal corner, and every junction with a bath, a tray or a worktop, gets a flexible sealant bead instead. It is not a cheaper finish, it is the right material for a joint that moves, and it is the commonest thing we find done wrong.

Tile onto something wet

A wall with a damp patch, a floor that has had water through it, or plasterboard that has gone soft behind a shower. The tile will stick for a while and then it will not. The water gets dealt with first, and that is sometimes somebody else's job before it is ours.

Tiling is not an emergency trade, with one exception

Almost nothing a tiler does cannot wait a fortnight. The exception is a cracked or loose tile in a shower or behind a bath where water is already getting past it, because that water is going somewhere and it is usually into the wall, the floor or the ceiling below.

Stop using that shower first

  1. Use another shower or a bath for now. Every use puts more water behind the tile.
  2. Run a temporary bead of sealant over the crack or the open joint if you have a tube in the house. It is not a repair, it buys you days.
  3. Look at the ceiling in the room underneath. A stain or a soft patch there changes this from a tiling job into something bigger.
  4. Ring and say which it is, so the right person comes.

What we will not do

Print a response time. There is no number on this site for how fast anybody turns up, because any number we put here would be made up. Ring and you will be told honestly when somebody can be there.

If water is coming through a ceiling, or the floor is soft underfoot, that is past a tiler. Get the water off first, then the damage looked at, then the tiling.
