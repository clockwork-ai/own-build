Houses round here

Why the straightedge comes out first.

Thick stone walls, and nothing plumb

A lot of the housing in this part of Cumbria is limestone or slate built and well over a century old. The walls are thick and solid and almost none of them are flat or plumb. Reveals are deep, corners are not square, and floors often fall towards the middle of a room. That decides the tile as much as taste does: a long format tile on a wall that bows will lip at every joint unless the wall is made flat first, and making it flat is real work with real materials. So the straightedge goes on before anybody quotes, and the honest conversation happens then rather than on the Wednesday.

Rain, and bathrooms in cold rooms

It is a wet corner of the country and a lot of these bathrooms were put into rooms that were never meant to be bathrooms, often on an outside wall with poor ventilation and no heating to speak of. Warm wet air against a cold outside wall gives you condensation, and condensation gives you black grout, soft plasterboard and sealant that never dries out properly. So we look at the extractor and the window before we look at the tile, and we will say plainly if the ventilation is the actual problem rather than the grout.

Tiling is not an emergency trade, with one exception

Almost nothing a tiler does cannot wait a fortnight. The exception is a cracked or loose tile in a shower or behind a bath where water is already getting past it, because that water is going somewhere and it is usually into the wall, the floor or the ceiling below.

Stop using that shower first

  1. Use another shower or a bath for now. Every use puts more water behind the tile.
  2. Run a temporary bead of sealant over the crack or the open joint if you have a tube in the house. It is not a repair, it buys you days.
  3. Look at the ceiling in the room underneath. A stain or a soft patch there changes this from a tiling job into something bigger.
  4. Ring and say which it is, so the right person comes.

What we will not do

Print a response time. There is no number on this site for how fast anybody turns up, because any number we put here would be made up. Ring and you will be told honestly when somebody can be there.

If water is coming through a ceiling, or the floor is soft underfoot, that is past a tiler. Get the water off first, then the damage looked at, then the tiling.
