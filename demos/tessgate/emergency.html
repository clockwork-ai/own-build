First, which problem is it

Tiler, plumber or something bigger.

This is a tiler

  1. A tile in the shower or behind the bath is cracked right across and the shower is still being used.
  2. A tile has come loose or is rocking, and there is an open joint round it.
  3. The sealant along the bath or the tray has come away and water is running down behind it.
  4. Grout has fallen out of a joint in a wet area and left an open gap into the wall.
  5. A tile has dropped off a shower wall altogether.

This is somebody else first

  • Water is coming through the ceiling below. Stop using the shower, get the leak found, then the tiling.
  • The floor is soft or springy underfoot near the shower. That is the structure, not the tile.
  • A tap, a mixer, a waste or a pipe is leaking. That is a plumber, and the tiling comes after.
  • The tray or the bath itself has cracked. The tray comes out before anybody tiles anything.
  • It is a flat and water is going into a neighbour's property. Tell the managing agent or the landlord first.

The three we get rung about

What each one actually needs.

A tile cracked right across

A single cracked tile is rarely about the tile. It usually means something behind it moved, or the tile was bridging a hollow and something heavy landed on it. In a dry room it can wait. In a shower it cannot, because the crack goes all the way through and water is going straight down it every time the shower runs. The repair is the tile out, the bed checked, the cause dealt with and a new tile in, and the honest part is finding out why it cracked rather than just replacing it.

Sealant come away from the bath

The most common of the three and the easiest to put right. The bead has shrunk, split or lifted along the bath or the tray, and water is running down the back of it rather than off it. Water behind a bath does not evaporate, it sits on the floor. The fix is cutting the whole bead out back to bare, cleaning and drying the joint properly, and running a new one in one pass, with the bath filled so the joint is at its widest.

A tile rocking or come off

If one has let go, tap the ones round it, because they are usually drumming too. A single loose tile in a shower wall is an open route into the wall. It wants taking out, the bed looked at, the background checked for damp and the tile refixed properly. If the wall behind turns out to be soft, that is a bigger job and we will say so rather than sticking it back on and taking the money.

Why we will not put a time on this page

Every firm you ring will say they can be there quickly. The honest answer is that it depends on where the van is, what is already on and how bad yours actually is. A plain number printed on a web page covers none of that, so there is not one here.

What you should get on the phone instead is three straight answers: whether this is a tiler's job at all, roughly when somebody could be there, and what you can do in the meantime. If a firm will not give you that on the phone, ring another one.

What to do tonight

Stop using that shower. Use another one or have a bath. Every use puts more water behind the tile, and the damage is done by repetition rather than by one go.

If you have a tube of sealant in the house, run a temporary bead over the crack or the open joint. It is not a repair and nobody should pretend it is, but it will keep most of the water out for a few days and it cuts out easily afterwards.

Then go and look at the ceiling in the room underneath, and at the floor round the shower. A stain, a bulge or a soft patch changes this from a tiling job into something bigger, and it is much better to know that before somebody turns up with a box of tiles.

Making it watertight is not the same as finishing it

There is a difference between a shower made usable again and a job finished properly. Sometimes they are the same visit. More often the first visit stops the water and the proper repair follows once a matching tile has been got hold of, or once the wall behind has dried out, which can take a fortnight on its own.

You should be told plainly which one you have just had, what is temporary and what is still outstanding. On a real site this is the page where that is written down, so nobody has to take it on trust from a stranger in their bathroom.

The matching tile problem

Worth knowing in advance. Dye lots vary between production batches, so a tile bought two years after the rest will not be quite the same colour, and on a plain pale tile that shows. If you have spares in the loft from when it was done, dig them out before anybody comes. If you have not, the honest options are a close match that is not perfect, or taking a tile from somewhere it will never be seen, behind the washing machine or under the bath panel, and putting the odd one there instead.

Questions

Asked at the bad moment.

Can you come out today?

On this demo nobody can, because the firm is not real and the number rings nobody. On a real site the answer depends on where the van is and what is already booked.

What a real firm should do is ask you three questions on the phone, whether water is still getting in, whether anything is showing below, and what has actually broken, and then give you a straight yes or no rather than a maybe that leaves you waiting in all day.

Is one cracked tile really urgent?

In a dry room, no. In a shower, yes, because the crack goes all the way through the tile and every shower sends water straight down it into whatever is behind. Days matter rather than hours, which is why the first thing to do is stop using it.

What makes it worse is that the water goes somewhere you cannot see. By the time there is a mark on the ceiling below, it has usually been going on for months.

Can one tile be replaced without redoing the wall?

Usually, yes. The grout round it comes out, the tile is broken out carefully from the middle so the neighbours are not disturbed, the old bed is cleaned off the wall, and a new tile goes in and gets grouted and sealed.

The two things that can change that are a matching tile, which is a dye lot problem rather than a skill problem, and what we find behind. If the background is soft or the tiles round it are drumming too, one tile is not the job.

Water is coming through the ceiling below. Is that you?

Not first. That needs the source found and stopped, and it may well be a plumbing leak rather than anything to do with the tile. Stop using the shower, get the leak traced, and get the ceiling looked at if it is stained or sagging.

Once it is dry and the cause is dealt with, the tiling repair is ours and we are happy to do it. Tiling over a wall that is still wet is money thrown away.

It is a rented flat. Who sorts this?

Tell the landlord or the managing agent straight away and in writing, and do it before you arrange anything yourself. Water getting into the structure of a block is their problem as much as yours, and in most tenancies it is theirs to fix.

Take photographs and note the date. That is not us being difficult, it is the thing that saves an argument later.
