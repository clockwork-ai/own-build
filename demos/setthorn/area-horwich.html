What we get asked for in Horwich

Mostly the same two things as everywhere: a drive that has had its day, and a patio somebody has been meaning to sort out for three summers. What varies from town to town is the houses and the ground under them. In Horwich it tends to be stone and brick terraces on a hill, so a lot of drives have a fall on them already and the question is where that water is going to end up.

So a visit here starts the same way every time. What is there now, which way does the ground fall, where can the water go, and is there anywhere to put the spoil while we work. Then the levels, and the awkward part found at the visit rather than on day two.

Doing it in stages

Plenty of people round here do the drive one year and the patio the next, and there is nothing wrong with that as long as somebody writes down exactly what went in the first time. The block, the colour, the pattern, the border and the jointing. Two years later that note is the difference between a back garden that matches the front and one that nearly does.

If that is the plan, say so at the visit. It sometimes changes the order we would do things in, usually because of access and where the muck can go.

This is not an emergency trade, and we will not pretend it is

Nobody is coming out at midnight to lay blocks. What we will do is get a trip hazard made safe quickly, then book the proper repair in daylight.

Make it safe first

  1. Mark it so people can see it. A cone, a bucket, a chair, a bag of sand, anything that stops somebody walking into it in the dark.
  2. If a flag is rocking, lift it right out rather than leaving it to tip under somebody's foot, and stand it against the wall.
  3. Keep the route to the front door clear, even if that means going round the long way for a few days.
  4. Take a photograph with something in it for scale, a shoe or a tape, before anything is moved.

If it is on the pavement rather than your drive

A lifted or broken footway flag outside your boundary belongs to the council, and it is their job to repair it. Report it to them. Do not pay anybody to mend the public footway.

Making safe is not the repair. A sunken patch is lifted, the layer underneath is put right and the same blocks or flags go back down. That gets booked properly rather than rushed in the dark.
