What we do

Drives, patios and what goes under them.

Six kinds of job, and they run into each other more than people expect. Somebody rings about a sunken corner, we find the water has been running under the drive from a blocked gully for three years, and the honest answer turns out to be a drainage job with a bit of paving at the end of it. Here is each of them on its own.

The six

One at a time.

Block paving driveways

Dug out properly, a sub base that carries a car, and blocks laid to a line. The bit you see is the last day of the job and the smallest part of it.

Tarmac and resin driveways

Tarmac where you want one quiet surface and a good edge. Resin bound where you want gravel that stays where you put it and lets water through.

Patios

Sandstone, porcelain or concrete flags on a full bed, falling away from the house, with the joints and the step down sorted before anybody sits on it.

Dropped kerbs, through the council

The crossing from the road on to your drive. The council decides whether you can have one and who may build it, so that part comes first.

Drainage for drives

Channel drains, soakaways, falls and permeable surfaces. Where the water goes is the question that decides most of a driveway job.

Driveway cleaning and repairs

Washing, re-sanding and sealing, sunken patches lifted and relaid, and the odd flag that rocks. Far more drives need this than need replacing.

What we do not do

Worth saying out loud.

Retaining walls holding up a bank

A low edging wall is part of the paving. Anything actually holding ground back wants somebody who can tell you what it is holding and how, and sometimes an engineer. Not us on our own.

Anything on the public footway without the council

Kerbs and footways belong to the highway authority. We will help you through the application, and we will not touch the public side of it unless the council says who is allowed to.

Laying over a drive that has failed

A smart surface over a broken base is two years of looking good and then the same problem, more expensive. If that is what somebody has quoted you, ask them what is underneath.

Not sure which of those you need?

Most people are not. Tell us what is there now and your postcode and we will work it out at the visit.

Urgent help

Sunken paving, or something somebody could trip on?

This is not an emergency trade, and we will not pretend it is

Nobody is coming out at midnight to lay blocks. What we will do is get a trip hazard made safe quickly, then book the proper repair in daylight.

Make it safe first

  1. Mark it so people can see it. A cone, a bucket, a chair, a bag of sand, anything that stops somebody walking into it in the dark.
  2. If a flag is rocking, lift it right out rather than leaving it to tip under somebody's foot, and stand it against the wall.
  3. Keep the route to the front door clear, even if that means going round the long way for a few days.
  4. Take a photograph with something in it for scale, a shoe or a tape, before anything is moved.

If it is on the pavement rather than your drive

A lifted or broken footway flag outside your boundary belongs to the council, and it is their job to repair it. Report it to them. Do not pay anybody to mend the public footway.

Making safe is not the repair. A sunken patch is lifted, the layer underneath is put right and the same blocks or flags go back down. That gets booked properly rather than rushed in the dark.
