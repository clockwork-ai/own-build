About

A firm that does not exist.

Culvermill Drainage is invented. There is no yard, no van and nobody to ring. It is a demo built by Clockwork AI to show a real drainage firm what a site like this looks like when everything on it is written properly. The story below is made up, and so is everybody in it.

The made up version

Three of us, one van and a camera reel. The name came from a culvert under an old mill race, which is the kind of thing you end up looking at when you spend your week following water downhill.

Most of our work arrives as a phone call from somebody standing over a chamber they have just lifted for the first time in fifteen years. So the way we work is built round that moment: tell us what you can see, stop using the water, and we will be straight with you about whether it is ours to fix, yours to pay for or the water company's to come out to.

How we would like to be judged

By whether the drain stays clear, and by whether you got told the truth about why it blocked. Clearing a drain is not difficult. Clearing the same drain three times in a year and never mentioning the crack that is causing it is the part of this trade that gives it a bad name.

So we put a camera down it. Not to sell a repair, because plenty of surveys end with us saying the pipe is fine and somebody flushed a wipe. To answer the question you are actually asking, which is whether it is going to happen again.

What we will tell you no about

Emptying a septic tank, which needs a licensed tanker. Anything to do with discharge rules, which is the Environment Agency and a specialist. Rats, which is a pest control trade. Gutters we cannot reach safely from the ground or a short ladder. And a repair priced without a survey, because that is a number with a guess behind it.

Stop using the water, then ring

Everything that goes down a sink, a bath or a toilet ends up in the same run. If that run is blocked, every flush and every washing machine cycle adds to what has nowhere to go. Stop using the water in the house, then ring and tell us what you can see.

While you wait

  1. Do not flush again to see if it has cleared. A second flush on a blocked run is how it ends up on the floor.
  2. Lift the chamber cover outside if you can do it safely, and look. Full to the top means the blockage is further down the run. Empty means it is between the house and that chamber.
  3. Keep children and pets away from anything that has come up, and keep your hands out of it.
  4. Move what you can off the floor and put towels at the bathroom door.
  5. Take a photo of the chamber. It is the quickest way for us to know what we are coming to.

If waste water is coming up inside the house, or if it is near sockets or anything electrical, treat it as unsafe. Switch that circuit off at the board if you can reach it from somewhere dry, and tell us when you ring.
