Fixing the pipe from inside it

Most of what used to mean a trench across the garden can now be done through the chamber. A resin soaked sleeve goes in, gets pushed to where the defect is, is inflated against the pipe wall and cures in place. What you end up with is a new pipe inside the old one, slightly smaller in bore and considerably better than what was there.

Patch or full liner

A patch is for one defect: a cracked joint, a short fracture, a hole where a root came in. It covers a length of around half a metre to a metre and leaves the rest of the pipe alone. A full liner is for a length that has had enough, where the defects are every few metres and patching them one at a time makes no sense.

Which one you need comes off the survey, which is why we will not quote a repair without one. Anybody pricing a liner off a description down the phone is guessing.

Roots

Roots are two jobs and people often only pay for the first. Cutting them out with a rotating head gets the pipe running again, and if you stop there they grow back, because the drain is still the wettest thing for yards around. The second job is closing the way they got in, which is a patch over the crack. Then they stop.

When we dig

When the pipe has collapsed, or when it is so deformed that there is no sound shape left to line against. Then a short section comes out and a new one goes in. We take up block paving carefully and relay it, lift and keep turf where it will survive, and put back what we took up. If a dig is the answer you will have seen the footage that says so before anybody starts.

On the day

How this one usually goes.

  1. Survey first, alwaysA repair is priced off footage and distances. No survey, no quote, because anything else is a guess with a number on it.
  2. Clean the pipe where the work goesThe liner has to bond to the pipe wall, so that stretch gets jetted properly first. Skip this and the repair lifts.
  3. Patch or liner, cured in placeResin soaked sleeve in through the chamber, inflated against the pipe, left to cure. Nothing comes up outside.
  4. Camera it again afterwardsThe same camera, the same run. You see the repair in place and the pipe running through it.
  5. Only dig if there is nothing to line toCollapsed or badly deformed pipe gets cut out and replaced. Pavers come up numbered and go back down after.

Questions

Asked about this job.

Does a liner make the pipe narrower?

Slightly, yes. You lose a few millimetres of bore to the thickness of the liner.

In practice it rarely matters, because the new surface is smooth and the old one was rough with scale and open joints. A smaller smooth pipe usually carries more than a bigger rough one.

How long does a lining repair last?

A long time, and we are not going to put a number on it on a demo web page because that would be an invented figure. What we will say is that a cured liner is not a temporary patch, and it is the standard fix for this kind of damage now rather than a bodge.

Can you repair a drain under a conservatory or an extension?

That is exactly what lining is for. Where a run goes under a floor slab, a conservatory or a driveway, a patch or a liner through the chamber means the floor stays where it is.

There are limits. If that stretch has collapsed completely then there is nothing to line to, and the conversation gets harder. That is one of the reasons a survey before you build over a run is worth doing.

Who pays if the damaged pipe is shared?

Depends where the damage is. Inside your boundary and serving only you, it is normally yours. Shared with neighbours, or past the boundary, and it is normally the water company's.

We give you the footage and the distances and tell you which side of the line we think it sits on. If it is theirs, we will say so rather than quote you for it.

Stop using the water, then ring

Everything that goes down a sink, a bath or a toilet ends up in the same run. If that run is blocked, every flush and every washing machine cycle adds to what has nowhere to go. Stop using the water in the house, then ring and tell us what you can see.

While you wait

  1. Do not flush again to see if it has cleared. A second flush on a blocked run is how it ends up on the floor.
  2. Lift the chamber cover outside if you can do it safely, and look. Full to the top means the blockage is further down the run. Empty means it is between the house and that chamber.
  3. Keep children and pets away from anything that has come up, and keep your hands out of it.
  4. Move what you can off the floor and put towels at the bathroom door.
  5. Take a photo of the chamber. It is the quickest way for us to know what we are coming to.

If waste water is coming up inside the house, or if it is near sockets or anything electrical, treat it as unsafe. Switch that circuit off at the board if you can reach it from somewhere dry, and tell us when you ring.
