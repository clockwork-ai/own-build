What we get asked for in Longridge

Mostly the same two things as everywhere: a gutter that has started going over, and a fascia board somebody has painted one time too many. What varies from town to town is the houses and how we get to them. In Longridge it tends to be exposed plots up on the hill where the wind gets at a loose downpipe and finds every bracket that was only ever screwed into the brick face.

So a visit here starts the same way every time. Which runs hold water, where the stains are on the wall, what the board behind the brackets is doing where we can see it, and whether a ladder can actually stand squarely under the run or whether it wants a tower.

Doing it in stages

Plenty of people round here do the front one year and the back the next, and there is nothing wrong with that as long as somebody writes down exactly what went on the first time. The profile, the colour, the bracket spacing and where the outlets are. Two years later that note is the difference between a back elevation that matches the front and one that nearly does.

The thing to weigh is the access, because that part does not get cheaper for being split in two. If the plan is stages, say so at the visit and we will tell you honestly which elevation genuinely needs doing first.

Nobody should be up a ladder in this

Not us and not you. In wind or heavy rain a ladder against a wet wall is how people get hurt, and a gutter overflowing for one more night does far less damage than a fall. The job tonight is to move the water away from the house and the people, and book the repair for the first dry morning.

What to do now

  1. Find where it is going over and put something under it. A bucket, a bin lid, a length of board leaning against the wall to throw the water out away from the brickwork.
  2. If a downpipe is hanging loose and you can reach it safely from the ground, tie it back against the wall so it cannot swing, and keep everybody out from underneath.
  3. Move anything that is getting soaked: bins, bikes, the dog bed by the back door, anything stacked against that bit of wall.
  4. Take a photograph or a short video from the ground while it is actually raining. Water tells you more in thirty seconds of rain than in an hour of dry weather.

If it is coming in rather than running down outside

Water showing inside, on a ceiling or down a chimney breast, is not a roofline job and may not be the gutter at all. Catch the drips, keep away from anything wet near electrics, and ring. We will tell you honestly if it wants a roofer instead, and we would rather say so than have a go.
