Urgent help

Gutter pouring over, or a pipe off the wall?

01632 960608

A demo number from the range Ofcom keeps for television and film. It rings nobody.

Nobody should be up a ladder in this

Not us and not you. In wind or heavy rain a ladder against a wet wall is how people get hurt, and a gutter overflowing for one more night does far less damage than a fall. The job tonight is to move the water away from the house and the people, and book the repair for the first dry morning.

What to do now

  1. Find where it is going over and put something under it. A bucket, a bin lid, a length of board leaning against the wall to throw the water out away from the brickwork.
  2. If a downpipe is hanging loose and you can reach it safely from the ground, tie it back against the wall so it cannot swing, and keep everybody out from underneath.
  3. Move anything that is getting soaked: bins, bikes, the dog bed by the back door, anything stacked against that bit of wall.
  4. Take a photograph or a short video from the ground while it is actually raining. Water tells you more in thirty seconds of rain than in an hour of dry weather.

If it is coming in rather than running down outside

Water showing inside, on a ceiling or down a chimney breast, is not a roofline job and may not be the gutter at all. Catch the drips, keep away from anything wet near electrics, and ring. We will tell you honestly if it wants a roofer instead, and we would rather say so than have a go.

No response times are promised anywhere on this site. On the real thing you would get a straight answer about the next dry morning rather than a figure invented for a web page.
