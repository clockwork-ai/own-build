Gutter repairs and replacement

Gutter repairs and replacement.

A dripping joint, a dropped bracket, a run that holds water in the middle. Most of it is a length and a seal rather than the whole set, and we will say which.

Finding it from the ground first

Before anybody goes up, stand back and look at the wall. A green or black stain in one strip under one spot is a joint or a bracket. A wash of staining along the whole run usually means the gutter is going over the front edge, which is a blockage or a fall problem. A stain at one end only often means the outlet. You can get most of the way to the answer from the pavement in dry weather, and it saves guessing.

Then up for a look. The two things we want to see are whether there is standing water in the run and what the fascia board behind the brackets is doing, because a gutter that has dropped is usually a timber problem wearing a plastic hat.

Why a gutter overflows

It is blockedMost likely, cheapest to fixLeaves on top, and underneath them the thing people do not expect: moss and the grit that washes off the tiles, settled into a heavy silt. It gathers at the low end and at the outlet, which is exactly where it does most harm. This is why a house with no trees anywhere near it can still block solid. Always rule this one out first, because it is a morning's work rather than a job.
The fall is wrong, or a bracket has droppedWater standing in the runA gutter should fall a few millimetres a metre towards the outlet. If it was never set with a line, or a bracket has pulled out of soft timber, water stands in a belly in the middle instead of moving. You can see it in dry weather: a tide mark and a line of silt where the standing water sits. Re-hanging that section on new brackets puts it right.
A joint or a seal has goneDrips rather than poursThe rubber seal inside a union flattens, hardens and gets grit behind it, and the two lengths either side move with the weather. It does not usually look dramatic, but it stains the wall underneath in one spot and that is how you find it from the pavement in dry weather. A new seal or a new union, and it is done.
There is more water than the gutter can takeThe one nobody checksA big steep roof pitching into a small half round gutter, or two roofs discharging into one run, or a long run with a single outlet at one end. In heavy rain it fills and goes over the front edge whatever you do. The answer is a deeper profile, a second outlet or a bigger downpipe rather than another clean out. It is the least common of the four and the one that gets cleaned four times before anybody measures it.

One length, or the lot

Gutter is lengths clipped into brackets and joined with sealed unions, which is the quiet advantage this trade has: a bad bit comes out and a new bit goes in. A cracked length, a split union or a sagging section is a repair, and a short one.

Two things push it the other way. The profile: some older shapes are not made any more, and a near match looks wrong and seals worse. And the age of the rest: if one joint has gone on a thirty year old run, the others are the same age and the same plastic. We will tell you plainly which of those two you are in rather than quoting the full set because it is easier.

What we will not do

Smear sealant round the outside of a leaking joint. It looks mended for a few months, traps the next lot of water behind it, and makes the real repair messy for whoever comes next. If a joint is leaking, the seal comes out or the union gets changed.

On the job

How this one usually goes.

  1. Look at the wall from the groundWhere the staining is tells us whether to expect a joint, a blockage, a dropped section or an outlet problem, before anybody climbs anything.
  2. Up for a proper lookStanding water in the run, the state of each union, whether the brackets are tight, and what the board behind them is doing.
  3. Say which it is, honestlyOne length and a seal, a section rehung on new brackets, or the run having had its day. With the reason, and a photograph where it helps.
  4. Do the repairSeal or union changed, or the section taken down, the brackets reset to a string line and the lengths clipped back in with the fall right.
  5. Water test itA hose or a bucket through the run while somebody watches the wall. A repair nobody has run water through is not finished.

Questions

Asked about this job.

Can you fix just one section?

Yes, and that is often the right job. A cracked length, a split joint or a dropped section comes out and goes back without touching the rest. What decides it is whether the profile still matches and whether the rest of the run is sound or is the same age and about to do the same thing.

My gutter only leaks in heavy rain. Is it still worth doing?

Yes, because that pattern usually means one of two specific things, and both get worse. Either there is a partial blockage that copes with drizzle and gives up in a downpour, or the gutter genuinely cannot take the volume off that roof. The second one is the only case where a bigger profile is the honest answer rather than a clean.

Can you match my old gutter?

Often, and we will be straight when we cannot. Half round and square are easy. Some older ogee and cast iron profiles are not made in a matching size any more. Where it cannot be matched we would rather change the whole run on one elevation so it looks deliberate than leave a near miss in the middle of it.

Is a sagging gutter the gutter's fault?

Usually not. It is normally the fascia board behind it going soft so the bracket screws have nothing to hold, or brackets set at too wide a spacing in the first place. Rehanging the same gutter on new brackets in bad timber just moves the problem along by a year, so that is the point where we talk about the board.

Nobody should be up a ladder in this

Not us and not you. In wind or heavy rain a ladder against a wet wall is how people get hurt, and a gutter overflowing for one more night does far less damage than a fall. The job tonight is to move the water away from the house and the people, and book the repair for the first dry morning.

What to do now

  1. Find where it is going over and put something under it. A bucket, a bin lid, a length of board leaning against the wall to throw the water out away from the brickwork.
  2. If a downpipe is hanging loose and you can reach it safely from the ground, tie it back against the wall so it cannot swing, and keep everybody out from underneath.
  3. Move anything that is getting soaked: bins, bikes, the dog bed by the back door, anything stacked against that bit of wall.
  4. Take a photograph or a short video from the ground while it is actually raining. Water tells you more in thirty seconds of rain than in an hour of dry weather.

If it is coming in rather than running down outside

Water showing inside, on a ceiling or down a chimney breast, is not a roofline job and may not be the gutter at all. Catch the drips, keep away from anything wet near electrics, and ring. We will tell you honestly if it wants a roofer instead, and we would rather say so than have a go.
