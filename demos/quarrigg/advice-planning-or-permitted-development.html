Planning or permitted development: the plain version

Two different routes, often confused. Here is the difference, in the order it matters.

These are two different things and people mix them up constantly, usually at the point where it costs them time.

Permitted development

Permitted development is a set of national rules that let you do certain work without applying for planning permission. There are limits on how far out you can go, how high, how close to the boundary, what the roof can do and what the outside can be finished in. There are extra conditions on some of it, and the allowances are different for a house and a flat.

It is not a free pass. If your house is a flat, is listed, sits in a conservation area or a national park, or has had its permitted development rights removed by a condition on a previous approval, the rules change or disappear. Houses on newer estates quite often have rights removed and the owners have no idea until they ask.

The useful thing to get

If the work really is permitted development, you can apply to the council for a lawful development certificate. It is not planning permission. It is the council confirming in writing that what you are doing does not need it. When you sell, a buyer's solicitor will ask, and that piece of paper saves an argument.

Planning permission

If the work goes beyond the limits, or the rights do not apply to your house, you apply for planning permission. The council looks at what it will look like, how it affects neighbours, light, overlooking, parking and the character of the street. There is a consultation period, neighbours can comment, and a decision follows.

Nobody can promise you an approval. Any builder who tells you it will definitely go through is telling you something they cannot know. What a good builder can tell you is what is normal round here and what tends to attract an objection.

Building regulations are separate

This is the bit that catches people most. Planning is about whether you may build it. Building regulations are about whether it is built properly: structure, insulation, damp, drainage, fire, ventilation, stairs. You can have permitted development rights and still need full building regulations approval, and you almost always will for an extension or a conversion.

The order that saves time. Work out the planning route first, then the drawings, then building regulations, then price it. Pricing a job before anyone knows whether it can be built in that shape is how quotes end up meaningless.

What to do next

  • Ring your council's planning team, or look at their website, and describe what you want.
  • Ask specifically whether your house has permitted development rights, and whether you are in a conservation area.
  • If it looks like permitted development, ask about a lawful development certificate.
  • Get drawings before you ask anyone for a firm price.

We are happy to say what we think you are looking at when we come out. The answer that counts still comes from the council.

While you wait

  1. Keep everyone away from anything loose or leaning, inside and out.
  2. If it is over a path, a drive or the road, block the way in so nobody walks under it.
  3. Do not prop it yourself and do not pull bricks out to have a look.
  4. If a vehicle hit it, or the wall is near a meter or a service, ring the right people first.
  5. Take a few photos from a safe distance and send them when we ask.

If anyone is hurt, or you can smell gas or see damaged cables, call 999 before you call us.
