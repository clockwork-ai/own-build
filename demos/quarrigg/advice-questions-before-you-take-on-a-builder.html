Questions to ask before you take on a builder

Eight questions worth asking any builder, including us, before you shake on it.

Ask every builder the same questions and the differences show up fast. Ask us them too.

1. Who is actually on site?

Is it the person quoting, their own team, or subcontractors they have not used before? There is nothing wrong with subbies, but you want to know who runs the job day to day and who you ring when something is not right.

2. What is in the price, and what is not?

Skips, scaffold, the structural engineer, building control fees, making good, plastering, decoration, taking the old wall away. A cheap quote is often just a shorter list. Put two quotes side by side and compare what each one leaves out.

3. What happens if you find something when you dig?

Old foundations, a drain in the wrong place, poor ground, asbestos in a garage roof. The honest answer is not "that will not happen", it is how it gets priced and who decides. Ask whether unknowns are a provisional sum and how a variation gets agreed in writing.

4. How do the payments work?

Stage payments tied to work completed are normal. A big deposit before anyone turns up is not. Ask what each stage is and what has to be finished before it is due.

5. Who deals with building control?

Somebody has to book the inspections and be there for them. Ask whether that is the builder or you, and what happens if a stage gets covered up before it is seen.

6. What is the programme, and what is it dependent on?

Not just how long, but what could move it: weather, lead times on steel or windows, the engineer's drawings, or a party wall award. A builder who has thought about it will tell you the three things most likely to slip.

7. Can I speak to somebody you worked for?

Not a review on a website, a person on a phone. Ask them what went wrong, because something always does, and what the builder did about it. That answer tells you more than ten good reviews.

8. What do you not do?

A builder who says they do everything either has a very big team or is about to learn on your house. We are straight about where our work stops and who picks it up.

One more, for yourself. Is it in writing? A quote, a list of what is included, and a programme. If it only exists as a conversation in your kitchen, it does not exist.

Written for this demo as general guidance. It is not advice on your house, and rules change. Check anything that matters with your council or a professional.

While you wait

  1. Keep everyone away from anything loose or leaning, inside and out.
  2. If it is over a path, a drive or the road, block the way in so nobody walks under it.
  3. Do not prop it yourself and do not pull bricks out to have a look.
  4. If a vehicle hit it, or the wall is near a meter or a service, ring the right people first.
  5. Take a few photos from a safe distance and send them when we ask.

If anyone is hurt, or you can smell gas or see damaged cables, call 999 before you call us.
