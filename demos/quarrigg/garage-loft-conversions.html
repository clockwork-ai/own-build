Services · Garage and loft conversions

The structural half of a conversion.

Lofts and garages turned into rooms. We do the part that holds it up and keeps the weather out: steels, floor, dormer or rooflight openings, blocking up the garage door, insulation and weathertight.

On the job

What happens

  1. Check what is up there now: joists, rafters, the head height and where the stair can land.
  2. Steels in to carry the new floor, set on padstones in the gable or party wall.
  3. New floor over the top of the ceiling below, so the ceiling does not crack.
  4. Dormer or rooflight openings formed, trimmed and made weathertight.
  5. Insulation, fire separation and the stair opening left ready for the joiner.

Questions

Common questions

Will the loft be high enough?

It depends on the roof. There is a rough rule about head height but the honest answer is that we measure it on the visit and tell you yes, no, or only if the roof is altered. We will not take a job on and find out later.

Does a garage conversion need building regulations?

Usually yes, because you are turning a cold store into a habitable room: floor, insulation, damp, ventilation and fire all come into it. Planning is a separate question and depends on whether you are changing the outside.

Do you fit the stairs and the bathroom?

We form the opening, carry the loads and get it weathertight. The stair, the plumbing and the fit out come from trades we work with, booked in behind us.

How disruptive is it?

Loft work is noisy over your head for a few weeks and there is a day or two when the stair opening goes in and the house is open to the loft. We tell you which days those are before we start.

Want somebody to come and look?

Tell us what you are after and where you are. We will come out, measure up and put it in writing.

While you wait

  1. Keep everyone away from anything loose or leaning, inside and out.
  2. If it is over a path, a drive or the road, block the way in so nobody walks under it.
  3. Do not prop it yourself and do not pull bricks out to have a look.
  4. If a vehicle hit it, or the wall is near a meter or a service, ring the right people first.
  5. Take a few photos from a safe distance and send them when we ask.

If anyone is hurt, or you can smell gas or see damaged cables, call 999 before you call us.
