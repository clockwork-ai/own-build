Self check · general information

Is it time for a new fuse board?

General information only, not a safety inspection. Only a qualified electrician can tell you for sure. Never take the cover off a consumer unit yourself.

What is a consumer unit?

It's the box, usually under the stairs or by the front door, where the electricity coming into your home is split into circuits: lights, sockets, cooker, shower and so on. Each circuit has a protective device, and modern boards add RCDs that cut the power quickly if current leaks somewhere it shouldn't.

If yours is old, it may still work, but it may not protect you the way a modern one would.

Common questions

Consumer units (fuse boards): questions

Do I have to replace my old fuse board?

Not always. If it's safe and suitable, it can stay. If an inspection finds it isn't, we'll explain why in plain English and quote for a replacement.

How long will I be without power?

Most of a working day for a typical home ${S}. We'll plan it so you know when.

What's an RCD?

A device that switches the power off very quickly if it detects current leaking to earth, for example through a damaged cable or a person. It's one of the most important safety features in a modern board.

Will everything work afterwards?

Usually yes. Sometimes a new board shows up an old fault that was hiding. If that happens we'll find it and tell you before we do anything else.

Safe first, while you wait

  1. If it's safe to reach, switch off the main switch on your consumer unit (fuse board).
  2. Don't touch damaged sockets, switches, cables or anything that's wet.
  3. Keep everyone, and pets, away from the area.
  4. Call us on the number above and tell us what you can see, hear or smell.

Fire, smoke or someone has had a shock? Call 999 first. Don't touch a person who is still in contact with electricity.
