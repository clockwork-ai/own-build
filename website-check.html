    Work I do for a client is covered by what we agree in writing before I start. For a new paid agreement, there is a 12-month minimum from the day the website actually goes live, once you have approved that go-live. After those 12 months it carries on month to month until one of us gives 30 days' notice in writing. If you stop during the 12 months, the payments still to come stay due one month at a time. I will not bring them forward, and I will not ask for the rest as one lump sum. The setup, what the monthly plan includes, and the price are agreed on the call and put in writing before I start. I host the website during the paid term. When notice ends, hosting and routine care stop, and you move the website away. The price you pay is the one in your written agreement. If a page and a written agreement ever disagree, the written agreement is the one we both follow. A written agreement can set a different term. If the monthly plan stops, what happens to missed calls, the chat box and review requests is in that written agreement.
    What stays yours
    The domain is in your name and stays yours. I hold access to look after it, nothing more. If you ever leave, it goes with you. It stays in your registrar account. You keep your Google profile, your social accounts, your phone, and your content and photos. You receive a copy of the website files you are entitled to, which are the pages and assets I built for you. You also receive a written list of accounts, renewals and settings needed to keep mail and the site working. I keep shared platform code, tools and intellectual property. This is not a blanket handover of all the code I use. The default is that file copy. I move the live project into an account in your name only if we agreed that in writing before we signed. There is no Clockwork fee for the agreed handover during the notice period. You pay any third-party costs, such as a new host or a domain or email charge. Accounts used for your business stay in your name. I work as a delegate on those accounts, and I keep a note of them. I do not treat those logins as mine. Your number stays your number. Nothing about how customers reach you changes unless you ask.
